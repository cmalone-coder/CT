# Surgical Treatment Plan — Consultation Summary (August 2026)

## Background

Patient has existing titanium hardware (plates and mesh) from prior craniofacial reconstruction and follow-up CT imaging was reviewed at this visit to plan the next stage of reconstruction.

## Findings at This Visit

- Existing titanium hardware (L-plates and mesh) is stable on CT, but is in direct communication with the sinus. Although the hardware is bio-compatible, it has become colonized by bacteria and needs to be removed.
- The hard palate bone on the right side is almost completely missing.
- An oroantral/oronasal fistula is present and requires repair.
- An incidental finding on CT — small, scattered material near the ear — was identified as likely fat necrosis, from previous fat grafting; not considered clinically significant and will be left alone unless it interferes with the surgical field.

## Proposed Treatment Plan

**Stage 1 — Bone reconstruction**
- Remove the existing titanium mesh and L-plates.
- Repair the fistula.
- Reconstruct the missing bony barrier between the oral cavity and sinus using a bone graft:
  - Preferred source: the patient's own bone (autograft) — specifically a split-thickness cranial bone graft, harvested from the outer table of the skull through a small scalp incision.
  - Alternative source if needed: cadaver bone (allograft).
  - The cranial donor site is filled with calcium hydroxyapatite bone cement, used as a contour filler rather than for structural support.
- New titanium plates placed to secure the bone graft during healing.
- Estimated healing time before proceeding to Stage 2: approximately six months.

**Stage 2 — Implant reconstruction**
- Repeat CT imaging after healing to assess the result.
- Virtual surgical planning based on the updated imaging, used to custom-fabricate a patient-specific PEEK implant.
- Implant placed and secured with titanium screws once the bone graft has established a stable barrier over the sinus.
